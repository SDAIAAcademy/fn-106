Session 1

Data classification levels

  • Assess the impact category and level of a data set's disclosure
  • Assign a classification level with an evidence-based reason
  • Spot aggregates that still reveal individuals
01
What classification is for

A classification level is a decision about handling, made once

  • Every data set gets a level when it is created or received.
  • The level decides access, storage, sharing, publication and, today, which model may see it.
  • Data not yet classified is treated as Restricted until it is.
  • The level is reviewed within a month of the first classification.

Whose policy?

The NDMO Data Classification Policy is written for public entities. Rawafid, a private group, adopts it in its own data policy; the PDPL binds it by law.

Sources: Data Classification Policy (NDMO, version 1, 2020): scope (public entities), principle 3 timely classification, section 1.4 (unclassified data is treated as Restricted), step 6 (review within one month). Be honest about the scope: the policy binds government entities. Many private organisations that work with government adopt it; Rawafid does so in this course, which is a choice of the case, not a legal claim.

Four levels

The level follows the impact of unauthorised disclosure

LevelImpactExample in the policy
Top SecretHigh: exceptionally serious, irreparableNational security information
SecretMedium: seriousInformation on vital installations
RestrictedLow: limitedPersonal identifiers, a medical file, individual transactions
PublicNonePublished statistics, public announcements

Restricted has sub-levels by scale: A a whole sector, B several entities or a group of individuals, C one entity or one individual.

Source: Data Classification Policy, section 1.3, Table 1 and the Restricted sub-levels. Point out where personal data sits: the policy's examples for Restricted include names, addresses, national ID numbers, phone numbers, bank accounts and medical files. A VIP's personal data can be Secret when it affects the national interest. School records are Restricted.

How to assess impact

Ask what disclosure would harm, then how badly and for how long

National interest

Reputation, security, economy, infrastructure, government functions.

Entity activities

Profits and functions of entities.

Individuals

Health and safety; privacy.

Environment

Environmental and natural resources.

The level weighs the duration of the damage, the time to recover and its scale. Every impact named must be specific and evidence-based.

Source: Data Classification Policy, Table 2 (categories and levels) and step 3 (impact category, then impact level: duration, recovery, size). For individuals' privacy the policy's guidance is: disclosure of an individual's personal information is a Low impact. That is the line Lab 2.1 uses.

The policy's process

A low impact still has two questions before it becomes a level

→
Step 4Would disclosure break a law? Yes: Restricted
→
Step 5Do the benefits outweigh the harm? Yes: Public. No: Restricted
Open by default. The two steps exist so that data is not locked away when nothing forbids its release.

Source: Data Classification Policy, steps 3b, 4 and 5, and principle 1 (open by default, for the development sector). For Rawafid's record sets step 4 answers yes at once: disclosure would breach the PDPL. For the fee schedule nothing is at stake. The interesting case is the summary table.

Combining data

A set built from several levels takes the highest of them

Principle 4 · highest level of protection

If a data set integrates parts with different levels, the highest level applies.

At Rawafid

The fee schedule (Public) joined with each family's balance (Restricted) is Restricted. So is a prompt that carries one Restricted field.

Source: Data Classification Policy, principle 4. This is the rule behind Lab 2.2's field-by-field decision: a prompt is a combined data set, so its level is the level of its most protected field.

Session 2

What may reach an external model

  • Derive a destination rule from the classification level and the data's nature
  • Apply the rule to data sets and to the fields of a prompt
  • Recognise a hosted endpoint abroad as a transfer outside the Kingdom
02
Where a prompt can go

Three destinations: a hosted service, a model you run, or no model

Hosted

A provider's model over an API. Fast to start; the data leaves you, under the provider's terms and location.

Locally served

An open-weight model on your own machines in the Kingdom. The data stays under your controls.

Neither

Some data has no business in a prompt at all. Minimisation decides before any model does.

This is the decision the catalog asks participants to make. A locally served model does not appear in the labs: the course runs only free hosted models on synthetic data, so nobody downloads model weights. The decision is made on paper here and is what Rawafid would need for real data.

What the guidelines say

Generative AI tools get Public data only

  • The Generative AI Guidelines for Government: do not enter data classified Restricted or higher into generative AI tools.
  • Limit their use to data classified Public; check the tool's privacy, disclosure and sharing terms.
  • Data analysis with generative AI is limited to Public or open data sets.

Rawafid's reading

The rule covers hosted services Rawafid does not control. A model Rawafid runs itself, under the source system's access rules, may see minimised Restricted fields.

Source: Generative AI Guidelines for Government (SDAIA, 2025), section 3.5 (privacy and security: no Restricted or higher, limit to Public) and the best practices on data analysis. The card on the right is the course's interpretation for a private entity running its own model; say plainly that it is Rawafid's policy choice, and that a stricter reading (no generative AI on Restricted data at all) is also defensible.

The rule as code

Three lines turn a level into a destination

def destination(level, has_sensitive):
    if level == "Public":
        return "hosted"
    if level == "Restricted" and not has_sensitive:
        return "local"
    return "neither"

Pseudonymised data keeps its Restricted level, so pseudonymising does not change the answer.

Walk through the four test cases of the lab: Public goes anywhere; Restricted without sensitive fields goes to a local model; Restricted with health notes goes nowhere; Secret goes nowhere. Ask: what would make you add a fourth branch? (A sector regulator's rule, for example health data in a hospital.)

The other half of the question

A hosted endpoint outside the Kingdom makes every prompt a transfer

  • Transfer only for listed purposes, without harm to national security or vital interests.
  • With an adequate level of protection abroad, and the minimum data needed.
  • Otherwise safeguards: standard contractual clauses, binding common rules or a certificate.

Location unknown?

Treat it as abroad. If you cannot show where prompts are processed, you cannot show the conditions are met.

Sources: PDPL Article 29; Regulation on Personal Data Transfer outside the Kingdom, version 2.0, Articles 2 to 4 (purposes, the adequacy list, safeguards and exemptions). The free model router used in class sends prompts to providers it chooses; the course treats its location as unknown and therefore as a transfer. That is fine for synthetic data and decisive for real data.

Why the class may use a hosted model

Synthetic records describe nobody, so they may go anywhere

In classsynthetic records, hosted free model
→
The gatebefore real records: classify again
→
In productionRestricted fields, local model only
What goes wrong. A prototype built on a hosted model is pointed at real data "just for a test". The test is the breach.

This is the course's own rule, stated in the catalog entry: a free hosted endpoint used only with synthetic data. Lab 2.2's stretch asks participants to write the check that must hold before anyone points Wafi at real records.

Session 3

Sharing with another entity

  • Assess a data request against the Data Sharing Policy principles
  • Cut a request down to the minimum data its purpose needs
  • Draft the conditions under which data may be shared
03
The request

A research centre asks for three years of attendance, with names

  • Purpose: commute distance and absence in primary and middle school.
  • Fields: names, national IDs, birth dates, district, guardian's employer, daily records, absence notes, health notes.
  • Storage: a cloud workspace outside the Kingdom.
  • Retention: indefinitely; onward: research partners when needed.

The decision

Not yes or no: what, in which form, on what conditions.

The request is in labs/data/partner_request.json. Al-Mizan Centre for Education Research is fictional. Ask the room for a gut answer before any instrument: most say no. The lab turns that into a reasoned yes with conditions, which is what the policy's sharing culture asks for.

The Data Sharing Policy

Eight principles decide whether and how data is shared

Sharing culture

Share by default, under the policy.

Single source

The entity that produces data shares it.

Legitimate purpose

A legal basis; used only for that purpose.

Authorised access

Identified, verified people.

Transparency

Data, level, storage, protection, destruction.

Accountability

Shared, under an agreement.

Security

Controls per the national rules.

Ethical use

Fairness, integrity, trust.

Source: Data Sharing Policy (NDMO), section two, the eight principles. The policy governs government data shared with requesters; Rawafid's policy adopts the principles for its own sharing, and the PDPL's disclosure rules apply by law. The agreement signatory depends on the level: for Restricted data, the director of the data management office.

The law's side

The PDPL allows research sharing when the data does not identify the child

Disclosure (Article 15)

Only in listed cases, among them consent, and later processing in a form that cannot identify the data subject directly or indirectly.

Research (Article 27)

Without consent when the data does not specifically identify the person, or identity evidence is destroyed first and the data is not sensitive.

The health notes fail both. They are sensitive and they identify; a commute study does not need them.

Sources: PDPL Articles 15, 16 and 27. Article 16 adds cases where disclosure is refused even when Article 15 allows it, including conflict with the interests of a person lacking legal capacity: a child.

The answer to the centre

Yes, on nine conditions

  1. The stated study only.
  2. The pseudonymised extract, coarse fields.
  3. Labelled Restricted in the metadata.
  4. Stored in the Kingdom, or a lawful transfer.
  5. Named researchers only.
  6. No onward sharing.
  7. Destroyed at the end, with a signed confirmation.
  8. A secure channel, not email.
  9. An agreement signed before any data moves.

The reference conditions of Lab 2.3. Three principles were "not met" in the request as written: authorised access, collective accountability and data security. Each condition answers one of them, or the PDPL. The stretch is the destruction confirmation.

Session 4

Licences and provenance

  • Read the licence of a model and of a dataset from its card
  • Record use restrictions and obligations in a licence register
  • Name the national instrument that decides a given question
04
What you reuse

Weights, code and data each carry their own terms

Weights

An open-source licence, or the vendor's own terms with a use policy.

Code

Usually a standard licence in the repository.

Data

Often Creative Commons, and with a provenance of its own.

Open weights are not public domain. Downloadable does not mean free of conditions.

Lab 2.2 concluded that Wafi needs a locally served model for real data. That raises the next question: which model, and which data to tune it on, under which terms. The course does not download the model: participants read cards and licences.

A shared vocabulary

An SPDX identifier names one exact licence text

LicenceSPDX identifier
Apache License 2.0Apache-2.0
Creative Commons Attribution Non Commercial 4.0CC-BY-NC-4.0
A vendor's own termsLicenseRef- and a name
  • A register, a scanner and a lawyer then mean the same thing.
  • Cards on model hubs carry a licence tag; read the licence file it points to.
  • Record the date you checked: terms change.

Source: the SPDX License List (version of 2026-10-28 checked for this course). LicenseRef- is the SPDX convention for a licence outside the list. Show the list page if there is a network.

Beyond the licence

Provenance asks where the content came from, and what came with it

  • Who made it, from what: a dataset generated by another model may carry that model's terms and errors.
  • Which languages: an English-only dataset will not teach Gulf Arabic replies.
  • What is inside: personal data in training text is still personal data.
  • When you checked, and where the record of it lives.

The national risk framework's data pillar mentions tracing data sources; the Generative AI Guidelines ask users to respect intellectual property. Day 3 measures what English-centred data does to Arabic users.

Licences in the other direction

Publishing open data means classifying it Public first, then licensing it

  • Open data is a subset of Public data: classify first, publish second.
  • Open by default, unless the data needs protection.
  • Machine-readable (CSV, JSON), free of charge, no registration.
  • Released under a licence that states the conditions of reuse.

At Rawafid

The fee schedule can be published today. The attendance summary only after its small cells are suppressed and it is classified again. The sports-day page was published with dates of birth: Lab 1.3 showed what that cost.

Source: Open Data Policy (NDMO, version 1, 2020): scope (unprotected public data produced by public entities), principles 1 (open by default), 2 (open format and machine-readable), 5 (non-discriminatory, no registration), 6 (free of charge) and 7 (a licence), and step 1 of the valuation process (identify the data sets classified Public). As with the other NDMO policies, it binds public entities and Rawafid adopts it. The point for a builder: you have just read other people's licences; when you publish, you are the one who writes the licence, and the classification register is what tells you which sets may be published at all. Ask: which Day 1 lab would have stopped the sports-day page? (Lab 1.3: the page needed age categories, not dates of birth.)

Yes, on conditions
