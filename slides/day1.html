01
The law's starting point

Personal data is anything that can identify a person, directly or not

  • Directly: a name, a national ID number, a mobile number.
  • Indirectly: a campus, a grade and a date of birth together.
  • Sensitive data is a named list in the law: racial or ethnic origin, beliefs, criminal and security data, biometric and genetic data, health data, and data showing a parent is unknown.
  • It applies to processing in the Kingdom, and from abroad for people living here.

At Rawafid

A note "asthma flare" on an absence is health data about a child. So is "insulin at lunch" in a student file.

Sources: Personal Data Protection Law, Article 1 (definitions of personal data and sensitive data) and Article 2 (scope). The AI Ethics Principles use a longer list that also names credit and location data; follow the law's own list when you classify. Do not read the list aloud in full; point to it. The key move for engineers: indirect identification. Session 3 proves it with a join.

Three roles

The controller decides, the processor acts for it, the data subject has the rights

RoleIn the lawAt Rawafid
ControllerDecides why and how data is processed; carries the obligationsRawafid Schools Group
ProcessorProcesses for the controller; chosen only if it gives the necessary guaranteesThe model provider, the hosting company
Data subjectThe person the data is about, or their legal guardianEach student, represented by a guardian; each guardian
A minor's rights are exercised by the guardian, and the guardian's authority must be verified.

Sources: PDPL Article 8 (choosing processors that give guarantees, and monitoring them); Implementing Regulation Article 13 (the legal guardian acts in the child's best interest, and the controller verifies guardianship). Ask: is the hosted model provider a controller or a processor? A processor, as long as it uses the prompts only to answer Rawafid. That is a contract question, which is why Lab 1.4 writes the provider's retention into the contract.

Where an AI build keeps data

Prompts, logs and caches are stores of personal data too

Records

The systems the build reads: students, guardians, attendance, fees.

Prompts

Everything the build sends to the model leaves your systems.

Logs

Often the full prompt and the reply, kept "for debugging", for ever.

Caches and queues

Replies kept for reuse; drafts waiting for staff.

What goes wrong. Teams map the database and forget the log. The log usually holds more personal data per row than any table.

Ask the room to name a system they built and where its logs go. Most logging libraries write whole request bodies by default. The model provider is a fifth store you do not control: its retention is set by its terms, which is why Lab 1.1's stretch adds it.

Session 2

Lawful basis and minimisation

  • Choose a legal basis and a purpose for each flow of a build
  • Test each field against the purpose and remove what it does not need
  • Rewrite a prompt builder so it sends the minimum
02
The rule and its exceptions

Consent is the default; the law lists when processing runs without it

Consent (Article 5)

No processing and no change of purpose without the data subject's consent, which can be withdrawn at any time. For a minor, the guardian consents.

Without consent (Article 6)

The data subject's actual interest when contact is impossible; another law or an agreement the data subject is party to; a public entity's security or judicial needs; the controller's legitimate interest.

The catch

Legitimate interest never covers sensitive data.

Sources: PDPL Articles 5 and 6; Implementing Regulation Article 16 sets the controls of legitimate interest (an assessment of purpose, necessity and the data subject's rights). Also Article 7: consent may not be made a condition of a service unless the service needs that processing. Ask: Rawafid's enrolment contract is signed by the guardian. Which basis covers answering their question about their child?

Wafi's five flows

Each flow gets its own basis and purpose

FlowBasisPurpose
F1 · answer the guardiancontractThe enrolment contract the guardian signed
F2 · draft with a modelcontractThe provider acts as processor for F1
F3 · keep a loglegitimate interestQuality and complaints, without sensitive data
F4 · cache replieslegitimate interestAvoid repeated model calls
F5 · add summer-camp offersconsentAdvertising needs prior consent and a way to stop

This is the reference of Lab 1.2, task 1. F5 rests on PDPL Articles 25 and 26 (advertising and marketing need consent; sensitive data never for marketing). The interesting row is F3: legitimate interest is possible only once the log no longer holds health notes, so the basis forces the minimisation. Reveal this slide after the lab if the room is quick; otherwise use it to set the lab up.

Minimisation

The purpose decides which fields may be collected and sent

  • The purpose must relate directly to the controller's purposes (Article 11).
  • Content is limited to the minimum the purpose needs.
  • Data not needed any more is no longer collected, and what was collected is destroyed.
  • Data is processed only for the purpose it was collected for, with listed exceptions (Article 10).

The guideline's test, per field

Actual need, purpose, method of collection, content, destruction, retention. If a field fails "actual need", it goes.

Sources: PDPL Articles 10 and 11; the Minimum Personal Data Guideline, first section (six principles) and its examples. Tell the guideline's example in one breath: a recruiter that asks every applicant for health data fails the test; an employer that keeps the blood type of field staff in dangerous work passes it. Necessity is about this purpose, not about possible future use.

The change in code

Send facts, not records

name = (student["full_name_ar"] if is_arabic(text) else student["full_name"]).split()[0]
data = {"first_name": name, **facts}   # three counts, nothing else
prompt = ("You are Wafi ... using only these facts.\n"
          f"Facts: {json.dumps(data, ensure_ascii=False)}\n"
          f"Guardian message: {text}")
Pattern. Compute what the question needs inside your systems; send the answer's ingredients, never the source rows.

Contrast with build_prompt_v1 in wafi.py, which dumps the whole record as JSON. The pattern generalises: compute counts and totals locally, pass them in. Ask the room what still is personal data in the new prompt: the first name and the counts, which are about an identifiable child. Minimised is not anonymous.

A school's special case

For a child, the guardian consents and exercises the rights

  • When a data subject lacks full legal capacity, the legal guardian acts in the child's best interest.
  • The controller takes appropriate steps to verify the guardianship before relying on it.
  • The guardian's consent may not harm the child's interests.
  • When the child reaches legal capacity, they exercise their rights themselves.
What goes wrong. Wafi finds the family by mobile number. A number shared between two families, or a parent without custody, gets another child's data.

Source: Implementing Regulation, Article 13. The planted problem behind the warning appears again in Lab 3.3 as harm H1. A Day 3 enquiry mentions a custody order: that one must go to a person, never to an automatic reply.

Session 3

Pseudonymisation and its limits

  • Pseudonymise identifiers with a keyed hash and keep the key apart
  • Attempt re-identification by joining on quasi-identifiers
  • Generalise quasi-identifiers and measure the remaining risk
03
Three words people mix up

Minimised, pseudonymised and anonymised are three different promises

Minimised

Fewer fields. What is left still identifies the person.

Pseudonymised

Identifiers replaced by codes; the extra information that links back is kept apart. Still personal data.

Anonymised

Irreversibly unidentifiable, directly and indirectly. Outside the law, once tested for re-identification.

Source: Personal Data Destruction, Anonymization and Pseudonymisation Guideline, sections two and three. The guideline says pseudonymised data remains personal data and protects less than anonymisation; anonymisation requires an impact assessment that evaluates re-identification. Ask: Dana took the names out. Which of the three did she do?

How to pseudonymise

Use a keyed hash, and keep the key away from the data

def code(value, key):
    mac = hmac.new(key.encode(), value.encode(),
                   hashlib.sha256)
    return "P" + mac.hexdigest()[:10]

out = df.drop(columns=["full_name",
                       "full_name_ar", "national_id"])
out["student_id"] = out["student_id"].map(
    lambda v: code(v, KEY))
  • Same person, same code: joins across files still work.
  • Without the key nobody can recompute a code.
  • A plain hash is not a pseudonym: anyone can hash every student number and look it up.

The stretch of Lab 1.3 shows the plain-hash failure: 1,200 student numbers are reversed in a fraction of a second. The lab key in data/lab_key.txt is for synthetic data only; in a real build the key lives in a secrets manager, not in the repository.

The attacker's tool

Quasi-identifiers link a record back to a name

Pseudonymised filecampus, grade, gender, date of birth, health notes
→
Joinon the four shared columns
←
Public pagesports-day results: name, campus, grade, gender, date of birth

No single column identifies a child. Together they almost always do.

The public page is Rawafid's own website: 320 names of children in races, with dates of birth for the age categories. The attack needs nothing but a dataframe merge. ARX is a full tool for this kind of test; in the lab a pandas join is enough.

Measured in Lab 1.3

A join re-identifies 96% of the names on the sports-day page

308 / 320
page names matched to one record
26

0

What goes wrong. "We removed the names" is the most common description of a file that is still personal data.

Numbers from the solution run: 308 of 320 page rows match exactly one record (96.3%), and 26 of those children carry a health note in the file. Count page rows, not names: two children can share a name. The date of birth does most of the work.

The fix, and its limit

Generalising cuts the attack to almost nothing, and leaves the rare child exposed

Exact birth date and grade
96%
Birth year and grade band
0.3%
  • Birth date becomes birth year; grade becomes a band of three.
  • One page name still matches one record.
  • The smallest group holds one child: k = 1.
  • 33 of 121 groups hold fewer than five children.

From the solution run: after generalisation 1 of 320 page rows is re-identified, k = 1, and 33 of 121 groups are under five. k-anonymity: every combination of quasi-identifiers is shared by at least k records. The guideline names generalisation and aggregation as techniques; the measurement is what tells you whether they were enough. Suppression of small groups is the next step, and Day 2 meets the same problem in an aggregate table.

The rule for any release

Claim anonymity only after you have tried to break it

1 · Removedirect identifiers
→
2 · Attackjoin with what is public
→
3 · Generaliseand suppress small groups
→
4 · Measurerate and k, written down
Your evidence for LO4: the before-and-after field list, the pseudonymisation script and the re-identification result.

The guideline asks the controller to evaluate the effectiveness of anonymisation techniques and adjust them, and to keep records of anonymisation and pseudonymisation activities. Lab 1.3's last task writes that record. In the capstone review the assessor may add a field (say, the bus route) and ask what happens to k.

Rights, retention and breaches

  • Explain the data subject's rights and who exercises them for a minor

04
What the data subject may ask

Five rights, answered within 30 days

  1. To be told the legal basis and purpose.
  2. To access their data.
  3. To obtain it in a readable, clear format.
  4. To have it corrected, completed or updated.
  5. To have it destroyed when no longer needed.
30 days
to act on a request, extendable by 30 with notice and reasons

For Wafi

A guardian's request covers the log and the cache, not only the student system.

Sources: PDPL Article 4 (the rights) and Article 21 (respond within the period the Regulation sets); Implementing Regulation Article 3 (30 days, one extension of 30 with prior notice and reasons). Ask: if a guardian asks for everything Rawafid holds about their child, where must Dana look? Every store on the Lab 1.1 diagram.

Retention

Destroy data once its purpose is met, unless a law says keep it

  • Destroy without undue delay once the purpose no longer needs the data (Article 18).
  • Keep it only for a legal basis with a set period, or a case before a court.
  • Data with nothing that identifies the person may be kept.
  • Destroy every copy, backups included, and tell those you disclosed it to.

A rule has three parts

Period (90 days), start (the date of the enquiry), method (scheduled deletion of rows and backups).

Sources: PDPL Article 18; the Destruction Guideline, first section (when to destroy, conditions, techniques such as overwriting and secure erasure). Where another law sets the period (financial records, education records), the course does not quote a number: the rule names who confirms it. Lab 1.4 writes the six rules.

The breach guide

A breach has three stages: notify, contain, document

1 · Notify SDAIAwithin 72 hours of becoming aware, when harm is expected
→
2 · Containstop it, change what can be changed, tell the data subjects
→
3 · Documentkeep the notice, the actions and the lessons
The clock starts at awareness, not on the day of the breach and not when the investigation ends.

Source: Personal Data Breach Incidents Procedural Guide (October 2024), and Implementing Regulation (72 hours). Notification goes through the national data governance platform, which requires registration: do that before an incident. The guide also says a report under the National Cybersecurity Authority's rules does not replace this one.

The record that ties it together

A record of processing lists what you have just mapped

  • Controller and DPO contacts; the purpose.
  • Categories of data subjects and of data.
  • Who receives it; transfers outside the Kingdom.
  • Retention period per category; security measures.

Kept in writing, up to date

Available to the competent authority on request, and kept five years after the activity ends.

Sources: PDPL Article 31; Personal Data Processing Activities Records Guideline (contents, five years, the template with mandatory and optional fields). The capstone asks for one record entry for Wafi: the diagram of Lab 1.1, the bases of Lab 1.2 and the rules of Lab 1.4 fill most of it.

Checkpoint

When must Rawafid have notified SDAIA?

The export went on the shared drive on 2026-10-21. Dana found the public link and told the DPO on 2026-11-09. Harm to the children is expected. By when must SDAIA be notified?

By 2026-10-24, three days after the upload
When the investigation is finished
Within 72 hours of 2026-11-09, so by 2026-11-12
Only if someone is shown to have misused the data
Why. The guide counts the 72 hours from the time the controller becomes aware, when the breach is expected to harm the data or the data subjects.

Answer: C. A is the most common mistake: the breach started earlier, but the obligation starts with awareness. Discuss briefly why D is wrong: the test is expected harm, and children's IDs and health notes on a public link meet it.
