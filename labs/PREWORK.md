# Pre-work: a reading guide to the core instruments

Do this before Day 1. It takes about three hours. You read short, named parts of ten national
instruments, answer one question about each while you read, and finish with an entry quiz of ten
questions. Nothing here needs a computer beyond a browser.

**AI policy for the pre-work.** An assistant may tutor you through an instrument, but check every
answer it gives against the official text: assistants invent article numbers and mix the law with
commentary about it. Take the entry quiz without an assistant.

All instruments are on SDAIA's regulations and policies page:
<https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Pages/RegulationsAndPolicies.aspx>. Read the English text; where
only the Arabic text is complete (the risk framework), the English summary is enough for now.

## How to read a legal instrument

1. Read the definitions first. Most disagreements are about what a word covers.
2. Read the scope: who is bound, and for what.
3. Then read only the parts named below, with the question in mind.
4. Write the article or section number beside each answer. In the course you will be asked "where
   does it say that?".

## Entry quiz

Answer without an assistant, then open the answers. Seven or more correct: you are ready. Fewer:
read again the instrument named beside each answer you missed. The quiz is for you; it is not
part of the course grade.

1. Which of these is sensitive data under the Personal Data Protection Law?
   a) A mobile number  b) A note about a child's asthma  c) A school campus  d) A fee balance
2. A controller wants to rely on its legitimate interest. For which data is that basis never available?
   a) Contact details  b) Data about employees  c) Sensitive data  d) Data kept in logs
3. A guardian asks for a copy of the data held about her child. Without an extension, the controller acts within:
   a) 72 hours  b) 7 days  c) 30 days  d) 90 days
4. A controller learns of a breach that is expected to harm the data subjects. It notifies SDAIA within 72 hours of:
   a) The day the breach happened  b) The time it became aware  c) The end of its investigation  d) The first complaint
5. Names are removed from a file and student numbers are replaced by codes; the controller keeps the key. The file is:
   a) Anonymous data  b) Public data  c) Personal data  d) Outside the law
6. Which list gives the four classification levels of the Data Classification Policy?
   a) Top Secret, Secret, Restricted, Public  b) Secret, Private, Internal, Open  c) High, Medium, Low, None  d) Critical, Sensitive, Internal, Public
7. Under the Generative AI Guidelines for Government, which data may be entered into a generative AI tool?
   a) Any data, if the tool is paid for  b) Restricted data, if names are removed  c) Data classified Public only  d) Any data on an official device
8. How many principles do the AI Ethics Principles set out?
   a) Four  b) Five  c) Seven  d) Ten
9. In the AI Ethics Principles, which risk level means a system is not allowed?
   a) Limited risk  b) High risk  c) Unacceptable risk  d) Little or no risk
10. The national risk framework gives entities a method for AI risks. In which order?
    a) Assess, identify, monitor, treat  b) Identify, assess, treat, monitor  c) Treat, monitor, identify, assess  d) Monitor, treat, assess, identify

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. b: health data is on the law's list of sensitive data (Article 1).
2. c: legitimate interest does not cover sensitive data (Article 6).
3. c: 30 days, which may be extended once with notice and reasons (Implementing Regulation, Article 3).
4. b: the 72 hours run from awareness (Implementing Regulation, Article 24; the breach guide).
5. c: pseudonymised data stays personal data (the pseudonymisation guideline).
6. a: Top Secret, Secret, Restricted, Public (Data Classification Policy, table of levels).
7. c: only data classified Public; nothing Restricted or higher (section 3.5).
8. c: seven.
9. c: unacceptable risk.
10. b: identify, assess, treat, monitor (the framework's summary).

</details>
